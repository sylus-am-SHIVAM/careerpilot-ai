# General Career Guidance

## Choosing a Career Path
Combine three things: what you enjoy (interests), what you are good at (aptitude) and what has opportunities (market demand). Use assessments as a starting point, not a final answer. Try short experiments before committing: online courses, small projects, internships and conversations with professionals in the field.

## Building Skills Effectively
Learn by building. After learning a concept, apply it in a small project the same week. Follow one structured course at a time instead of many in parallel. Practise consistently, for example one to two focused hours daily. Keep notes and share your learning publicly through GitHub, a blog or LinkedIn.

## Internships
Internships give real experience and often lead to job offers. Apply early and widely, use college placement cells, LinkedIn, Internshala and company career pages. A short, specific cover message and a one-page resume that highlights projects improve response rates.

## Resume Basics
Keep a fresher resume to one page. Include education, skills, projects (with what you built, the tech used and the impact), internships, achievements and links (GitHub, portfolio, LinkedIn). Use action verbs and numbers where possible. Tailor the resume to each role by matching the important keywords from the job description.

## Interview Preparation
Understand the role's core skills, practise explaining your projects clearly (problem, approach, your contribution, results, what you would improve), practise common questions, and do mock interviews with friends or seniors.

## Higher Studies vs Job
Higher studies make sense when the target role requires it (research, academia, some specialised fields) or when you want to switch domains. A job first gives experience and clarity, and many people pursue higher studies later. Consider cost, interest in the subject and long-term goals.
