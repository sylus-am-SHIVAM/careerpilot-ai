# Social, Teaching & Healthcare Careers

## Teacher and Educator
Teachers help students learn through classroom teaching, online courses or tutoring.
Core skills: subject knowledge, communication, lesson planning, patience, classroom management, using digital teaching tools.
Learning path: strong subject foundation, a teaching qualification, practice through tutoring or volunteering, then classroom or online teaching experience.
Entry routes in India: B.Ed after graduation is required for most school teaching roles; CTET/state TETs are used for eligibility. Online teaching and EdTech content creation are growing alternatives.
Portfolio ideas: recorded sample lessons, a YouTube or blog explaining concepts, tutoring testimonials.

## Psychologist and Counsellor
Psychologists and counsellors support people's mental health, wellbeing and personal growth.
Core skills: empathy, active listening, ethics, psychological assessment, counselling techniques, research methods.
Learning path: BA/B.Sc in Psychology, then MA/M.Sc in Psychology (clinical, counselling or organisational), supervised practice, and further qualification for clinical work.
Entry routes in India: clinical psychologists need an M.Phil/Professional Diploma or equivalent programme recognised by the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI). Counselling roles exist in schools, colleges, companies and NGOs.
Free resources: introductory psychology courses on NPTEL, Coursera (audit) and Khan Academy.

## Doctor and Healthcare Professional
Healthcare professionals diagnose, treat and care for patients. Besides doctors, roles include nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy, public health and allied health sciences.
Core skills: biology and chemistry, clinical knowledge, empathy, communication, decision making under pressure.
Entry routes in India: MBBS and many medical programmes require NEET-UG. Nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy and allied health have their own degrees. Public health (MPH) suits people interested in health systems and policy.
Advice: medicine is a long path; speak to practising professionals and consider allied health options that match your interests.

## HR Specialist
Human resources specialists hire, develop and support employees and shape workplace culture.
Core skills: communication, recruitment, employee relations, labour law basics, HR tools, data-driven decision making.
Learning path: any graduation (BBA or psychology helps), then an MBA/PGDM in HR or a specialised HR course, internships in recruitment or HR operations.
Portfolio ideas: internship projects, a case study on employee engagement, certifications in HR analytics.
